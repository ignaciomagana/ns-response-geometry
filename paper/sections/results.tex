\section{Local response hierarchy}
\label{sec:localresults}

The first central test compares the EOS response transverse to the stellar sequence in the I--Love and C--Love planes. Both are constructed from the same stellar configurations and EOS metric, and no empirical relation is used to define either normal direction.

For each reference configuration we compute the covariance-weighted normal response. In a two-observable plane the normal cotangent direction is unique once its normalization metric is declared. If \(G^{AB}\) is the EOS-induced covariance, \(t^A\) the sequence tangent, and \(g_{AB}\) the observable metric, the scalar response is
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_\perp^2
    =
    n_A G^{AB}n_B,
    \qquad
    n_A t^A=0,
    \qquad
    n_A g^{AB}n_B=1.
    \label{eq:normal-rms}
\end{equation}
The same EOS covariance is used when comparing I--Love and C--Love.

\subsection{Development diagnostic}

Before constructing a broad EOS ensemble, we performed a deliberately restricted diagnostic to determine whether the proposed response geometry contains the expected signal at all. The reference model is a relativistic \(\Gamma=2\) polytrope with \(K=100\) in geometrized units. Five Gaussian latent sound-speed modes span \(h\in[0.02,0.55]\). Their coefficient covariance is Eq.~\eqref{eq:eos-covariance} with \(\sigma_a=0.1\) and \(\ell_h=0.08\). We use the Euclidean metric in \((\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\) for this diagnostic only.

Table~\ref{tab:pilot-response} reports the resulting local normal RMS responses. Across the tested sequence, the I--Love normal response is smaller than the C--Love response by a factor between \(7.5\) and \(10.8\) in RMS, or roughly \(57\)--\(116\) in variance. This calculation uses one reference EOS, one EOS covariance, one basis resolution, and one observable metric and therefore is not yet evidence for prior-independent universality. Its purpose is narrower: the differential construction recovers a strong hierarchy without inserting an empirical I--Love fit.
