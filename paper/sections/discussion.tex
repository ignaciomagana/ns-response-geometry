\section{Discussion}

The response geometry separates three statements that are combined in an empirical universal relation. First, EOS perturbations may excite one normal observable-space direction much less than another. Second, the local soft covectors must be approximately integrable to define a common global surface. Third, finite departures from the local soft tangent plane acquire a controlled nonlinear contribution once the first-order term is suppressed. The calculations above support these statements in those specific senses for \((C,\bar I,\Lambda_2)\) over the tested cold, causal EOS domain. The first statement is closely related to recent linear-response analyses of fixed universal relations \cite{hu2026linear} and to asymptotic reductions of the stellar equations \cite{kamata2026asymptotic}; the distinction here is that the weakly excited normal direction is inferred from the full metric-weighted response operator before a relation is chosen, and is then tested for integrability and nonlinear curvature.

The central result is stronger than the observation that the I--Love band is thin. After displacement along a stellar sequence is removed, the full three-observable response possesses a separated normal spectrum. Across the broad finite-background ensemble, the median soft-to-hard normal RMS ratio is \(0.0204\), with 90th percentile \(0.0469\). The corresponding covector is nearly the I--Love normal, with median alignment \(0.9977\), but it carries a small compactness component. This compactness dependence should not be confused with discovery of a new I--Love--C relation: analytic and numerical I--Love--C relations are already established \cite{jiang2020ilovec}. Here compactness enters because the local low-response direction is inferred from the full EOS-response operator rather than chosen in advance.

The explicit EOS covariance makes one source of apparent universality transparent. Scatter measured over a finite EOS catalog implicitly assigns a distribution to EOS variations. In the present construction that weighting is exposed as \(C_{\rm EOS}\). The I--Love-like hierarchy survives changes in the covariance kernel, correlation length, nodal resolution, reference EOS, and observable normalization metric considered here. It is therefore not tied to one discrete set of named EOSs, although the numerical response scales remain metric dependent by construction.

The integrability result connects local stationarity to a global relation. The Frobenius obstruction remains at the \(10^{-3}\) level over the high-resolution test patches, while a scalar potential inferred only from local gradient information generalizes to unseen EOS backgrounds. The potential is useful here as a diagnostic of the soft distribution, not as a preferred new fitting formula. In particular, the improvement over a one-dimensional I--Love reconstruction demonstrates that the compactness component of the local soft mode is coherent across EOSs rather than random noise.

The curvature calculation gives a finite radius to this geometric picture. For sufficiently small EOS displacements the quadratic response predicts the normal departure at percent-level accuracy, and at moderate amplitude the curvature term becomes the dominant contribution to the residual. At larger deformation the second-order expansion fails, as expected. This distinction is important: first-order stationarity does not imply negligible scatter for arbitrarily broad EOS priors.

The controlled softening experiment also changes the interpretation of ``breakdown.'' Sharp phase transitions are already known to degrade the accuracy of tidal universal relations \cite{han2019phasetransition}, while useful universal behavior may persist in models with exotic or hybrid cores \cite{yip2026elastic}. Our response calculation resolves these statements: a localized soft layer can rotate the low-response covector away from the ordinary I--Love projection without eliminating the separation of the full normal spectrum. In the refined example, the covariance-weighted I--Love variance increases by a factor \(85\), yet the soft-to-hard normal RMS ratio remains \(0.096\). More than \(90\%\) of the added direct I--Love sensitivity is localized on the imposed layer. The geometry therefore distinguishes failure of a chosen two-observable projection from failure of low-dimensional EOS response itself.

The transition-location experiment should not be turned into a universal-shell claim. The fixed-depth \(\Gamma=2\) scan shows that the response formalism can localize the EOS perturbation responsible for rotating the soft mode, but preliminary cross-EOS controls were rejected after a branch audit exposed an invalid reference configuration and a non-monotonic depth response. We therefore separate the valid localization demonstration from any cross-EOS statement; the latter is made only from the branch-safe control.

This density-resolved behavior connects naturally with earlier physical accounts of I--Love--Q universality based on self-similarity, stiffness, and the dominant contribution of the stellar outer layers \cite{yagi2014why,sham2015unveiling}. The present framework does not replace those explanations. It supplies a differential diagnostic that can locate which EOS perturbations excite the otherwise soft observable direction and can test whether the resulting rotated distribution remains integrable.

Several limitations are deliberate. We consider cold barotropic stars in general relativity, static tides, and first-order slow rotation. The broad latent-field ensemble is a function-space stress test rather than a nuclear-physics posterior. The transition profiles are controlled softening experiments rather than full microphysical first-order phase-transition constructions, and the positive-\(dM/dh_c\) screen used in the transition scans is not a direct radial-mode stability calculation. Finally, observable-space norms require a declared metric; only statements invariant under coordinate changes with the metric transformed consistently are geometric.

\section{Conclusions}

We have recast neutron-star quasi-universal relations as low-response structure of the relativistic EOS-to-observable map. With an explicit metric on EOS function space, removal of the stellar-sequence tangent exposes a strongly separated soft normal mode in \((\ln C,\ln\bar I,\ln\Lambda_2)\). The mode is robustly I--Love-like but not exactly confined to the I--Love plane. Its covector field is approximately integrable across large causal EOS deformations, and the corresponding scalar potential generalizes across held-out EOS backgrounds when reconstructed from gradients alone. Second-order curvature quantitatively predicts the initial finite departure from the local soft tangent plane.

Localized EOS softening provides the complementary breakdown test. It can rotate the soft covector enough to substantially degrade I--Love while the full normal hierarchy survives, and the excess sensitivity is localized on the responsible stellar layer. Quasi-universality is therefore usefully described not only by the scatter of a fitted relation, but by the rank, orientation, integrability, and curvature of the EOS response itself.
