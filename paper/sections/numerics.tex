\section{Numerical implementation and validation}
\label{sec:numerics}

The production stellar calculation uses a fixed-step fourth-order
Runge--Kutta integration in relativistic enthalpy.  Fixed control flow keeps
the EOS-to-observable map differentiable and makes the derivative calculation
transparent.  The background, static \(l=2\) tidal perturbation, and
first-order frame-dragging equations are evolved together.  An independent
adaptive calculation in the radius coordinate, using DOP853, is retained
only for validation.

Automatic differentiation is used to evaluate the finite-dimensional
Jacobian \(J^A{}_i\).  It is a computational method rather than a validation.
Every production derivative is checked against symmetric finite differences,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial y^A}{\partial a_i}
    \simeq
    \frac{
      y^A(a_i+\Delta a_i)
      -
      y^A(a_i-\Delta a_i)}
      {2\Delta a_i},
    \label{eq:fd}
\end{equation}
over a range of step sizes.

\subsection{Stellar-structure validation}

The background integrator is first checked against the exact
Schwarzschild-interior solution for a constant-energy-density star.  Using
8192 enthalpy steps and the regular center offset
\(\Delta h/h_c=10^{-4}\), the relative errors in \(M\), \(R\), and \(C\)
are at the \(10^{-6}\) level over the three central enthalpies listed in
Table~\ref{tab:background-validation}.

the exact constant-energy-density solution.}
\label{tab:background-validation}

\begin{tabular}{cccc}

\hline
0.05 & \(-2.16\times10^{-6}\) & \(-7.23\times10^{-7}\) &
\(-1.44\times10^{-6}\)\\
0.15 & \(-1.19\times10^{-6}\) & \(-4.00\times10^{-7}\) &

The full observable solver is then compared against an independently coded
radius-coordinate adaptive integration.  This cross-check shares the
physical equations but not the integration variable or numerical stepping.
For a constant-energy-density configuration at \(h_c=0.15\), the maximum
absolute relative difference among
\[
(M,R,C,k_2,\Lamtwo,I,\Ibar,I/MR^2)
\]
is \(4.71\times10^{-7}\).  For a relativistic \(\Gamma=2\) polytrope at
\(h_c=0.10\), the corresponding maximum difference is
\(8.10\times10^{-7}\).  The largest discrepancy in both comparisons is in
\(\Lamtwo\), whose \(C^{-5}\) dependence amplifies otherwise smaller
differences in the background solution.

As a separate low-compactness check, the homogeneous-star solution at
\(C=4.96\times10^{-3}\) gives
\[
k_2=0.72897,\qquad I/(MR^2)=0.40171,
\]
approaching the Newtonian values \(3/4\) and \(2/5\), respectively.  A
relativistic \(\Gamma=2\) polytrope at
\(C=2.49\times10^{-3}\) gives
\[
k_2=0.25543,\qquad I/(MR^2)=0.26206,
\]
compared with the Newtonian \(n=1\) values
\[
k_2=\frac{15-\pi^2}{2\pi^2}=0.25991,\qquad
\frac{I}{MR^2}
=\frac{2}{3}\frac{\pi^2-6}{\pi^2}=0.26138.
\]
These finite-compactness differences have the expected sign and scale and
vanish as the central enthalpy is lowered.

\subsection{Derivative and response validation}

The response calculation is accepted only after the following additional
tests:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item automatic derivatives agree with symmetric finite differences;
    \item the induced response converges as the nodal latent-field grid is
    refined while holding the target functional covariance fixed;
    \item the I--Love versus C--Love hierarchy is repeated with more than one
    EOS covariance kernel and correlation length;
    \item the hierarchy is repeated about more than one reference EOS before
    moving to a broad nonparametric ensemble;
    \item local response scalars are invariant under observable-coordinate
    transformations when the observable metric is transformed consistently;
    \item the result is stable to the enthalpy integration and EOS
    reconstruction resolution.
\end{enumerate}

The observable-coordinate test is important because a numerical measure of
thinness can change under reparameterization if the normal covector is
normalized inconsistently.  We therefore transform \(G^{AB}\), \(t^A\), and
\(g_{AB}\) as tensors and covariant metrics, rather than comparing Euclidean
norms in unrelated coordinate systems.

The initial Gaussian-mode diagnostic used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:localresults} predates the nodal production representation.  It
is retained as a reproducibility check only.  All robustness and later
science results use nodal values of the latent sound-speed field, for which
grid refinement corresponds to a fixed functional EOS covariance.
