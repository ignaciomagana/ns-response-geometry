\section{Local response hierarchy}
\label{sec:localresults}

The first central test compares the EOS response transverse to the stellar sequence in the I--Love and C--Love planes. Both are constructed from the same stellar configurations and EOS metric, and no empirical relation is used to define either normal direction.

For each reference configuration we compute the covariance-weighted normal response. In a two-observable plane the normal cotangent direction is unique once its normalization metric is declared. If \(G^{AB}\) is the EOS-induced covariance, \(t^A\) the sequence tangent, and \(g_{AB}\) the observable metric, the scalar response is
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_\perp^2
    =
    n_A G^{AB}n_B,
    \qquad
    n_A t^A=0,
    \qquad
    n_A g^{AB}n_B=1.
    \label{eq:normal-rms}
\end{equation}
The same EOS covariance is used when comparing I--Love and C--Love.

\subsection{Development diagnostic}

Before constructing a broad EOS ensemble, we performed a deliberately restricted diagnostic to determine whether the proposed response geometry contains the expected signal at all. The reference model is a relativistic \(\Gamma=2\) polytrope with \(K=100\) in geometrized units. Five Gaussian latent sound-speed modes span \(h\in[0.02,0.55]\). Their coefficient covariance is Eq.~\eqref{eq:eos-covariance} with \(\sigma_a=0.1\) and \(\ell_h=0.08\). We use the Euclidean metric in \((\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\) for this diagnostic only.

Table~\ref{tab:pilot-response} reports the resulting local normal RMS responses. Across the tested sequence, the I--Love normal response is smaller than the C--Love response by a factor between \(7.5\) and \(10.8\) in RMS, or roughly \(57\)--\(116\) in variance. This calculation uses one reference EOS, one EOS covariance, one basis resolution, and one observable metric and therefore is not yet evidence for prior-independent universality. Its purpose is narrower: the differential construction recovers a strong hierarchy without inserting an empirical I--Love fit.

\subsection{Production nodal response and robustness}

We next repeat the calculation using nodal latent-field values,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nodal-coordinates}, so that increasing the number of EOS
coordinates refines a fixed functional covariance.  At \(h_c=0.16\) for the
\(\Gamma=2\) reference sequence, the I--Love/C--Love normal RMS ratio is
\[
0.10102,\quad 0.10286,\quad 0.10279,\quad 0.10278
\]
for \(5,9,17,\) and \(33\) nodes, respectively.  The 17-node result differs
from the 33-node value by \(3.6\times10^{-5}\) fractionally.  We use 17 nodes
for the remaining robustness tests.

For the Euclidean metric in logarithmic observables, changing the EOS
covariance among squared-exponential, Mat\'ern-\(3/2\), and exponential
kernels and varying \(\ell_h=0.04,0.08,0.16\) gives
\begin{equation}
    0.1024
    \le
    \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
         {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}
    \le
    0.1034.
    \label{eq:kernel-robustness}
\end{equation}
Thus the order-of-magnitude suppression is insensitive to both the
correlation length and the differentiability of the allowed EOS
perturbations in this controlled reference problem.

Changing the observable normalization metric changes the numerical response
scale, as it must.  A metric that weights the \(\ln\Ibar\) direction four
times more strongly gives RMS ratios \(0.183\)--\(0.185\), while weighting
the \(\ln\Lamtwo\) direction gives \(0.1048\)--\(0.1058\).  The hierarchy
therefore persists under these deliberately nonuniform normalizations,
although the numerical ratio itself is metric dependent.  This should be
distinguished from a coordinate transformation: transforming from
\((\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\) to
\((C,\Ibar,\Lamtwo)\) while transforming \(G^{AB}\), \(t^A\), and \(g_{AB}\)
consistently changes the I--Love and C--Love variances by only
\(8.4\times10^{-14}\) and \(9.4\times10^{-16}\) fractionally, respectively.

The hierarchy is also present away from the \(\Gamma=2\) reference.  For
smooth causal relativistic polytropes with
\(\Gamma=1.70,1.85,2.00\) and \(h_c=0.12,0.20,0.30\), spanning
\(C=0.073\)--\(0.171\), we find
\begin{equation}
    0.096
    \le
    \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
         {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}
    \le
    0.207.
    \label{eq:reference-robustness}
\end{equation}
Even the weakest case therefore retains a factor \(4.8\) suppression in
RMS response.

\subsection{Full normal spectrum}

The two-dimensional comparison above tests a chosen projection of the
observable space.  The response-geometry formulation makes a stronger
prediction in the full three-dimensional space
\((\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\).  After quotienting the stellar-sequence
tangent, two independent normal directions remain.  We denote their
EOS-induced variances by
\[
    \lambda_{\rm soft}\le\lambda_{\rm hard}
\]
and characterize the hierarchy by
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R_{\rm N}
    \equiv
    \sqrt{
      \frac{\lambda_{\rm soft}}
           {\lambda_{\rm hard}}
    }.
    \label{eq:normal-spectrum-ratio}
\end{equation}

For the \(\Gamma=2\) reference sequence,
\(\mathcal R_{\rm N}\) increases from \(2.2\times10^{-3}\) at
\(C=0.067\) to \(5.1\times10^{-2}\) at \(C=0.171\).  Thus the quotient
response is itself strongly anisotropic: one normal direction is between
roughly \(20\) and \(460\) times quieter in RMS than the other over this
sequence.

To identify this soft direction, let \(n_A^{\rm soft}\) be its normalized
covector and let \(n_A^{I{\rm Love}}\) be the I--Love plane normal embedded
in the full observable space with zero compactness component.  We define
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal A_{I{\rm Love}}
    =
    \left|
      n_A^{\rm soft}
      g^{AB}
      n_B^{I{\rm Love}}
    \right|
\end{equation}
for unit covectors in the baseline observable metric.  Along the reference
sequence,
\[
    0.9924\le \mathcal A_{I{\rm Love}}\le0.9958.
\]
The full soft mode is therefore not an unrelated three-observable
combination.  It is nearly the I--Love normal, with a small compactness
component.  At \(C=0.115\), for example,
\begin{equation}
    n^{\rm soft}
    \simeq
    (0.099,-0.947,0.304),
\end{equation}
in the coordinate ordering
\((\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\), while the embedded I--Love normal is
approximately
\[
    (0,-0.957,0.291).
\]

The same structure persists when the expansion point is moved through the
broad latent-field ensemble described below.  Across the 44 stable
backgrounds,
\begin{equation}
    {\rm median}(\mathcal R_{\rm N})=0.0204,
    \qquad
    q_{0.90}=0.0469,
    \qquad
    \max\mathcal R_{\rm N}=0.0883,
    \label{eq:broad-normal-spectrum}
\end{equation}
while the median I--Love alignment is \(0.9977\).  Restricting to
\(C\ge0.05\), the largest normal-mode ratio is \(0.0797\), the minimum
alignment is \(0.9756\), and the median alignment is \(0.9979\).
The principal geometric result is therefore that, after removing motion
along the stellar sequence, the relativistic EOS response contains a
distinguished soft normal mode that is robustly I--Love-like over large
functional EOS deformations.

\subsection{Broad nonparametric background ensemble}

We next move the expansion point itself through EOS function space.  Starting
from the same low-density anchor, we draw 16 finite latent sound-speed
backgrounds with unit RMS-scale perturbations: eight from a smooth
squared-exponential process with \(\ell_h=0.08\) and eight from a rougher
Mat\'ern-\(3/2\) process with \(\ell_h=0.04\).  Each background remains causal
by construction.  The local response Jacobian is recomputed about the
nonzero field at \(h_c=0.12,0.20,0.28\).  Four of the 48 configurations lie
on a negative-\(dM/dh_c\) branch and are excluded from the stable-star
summary.

At the fine \(2048\)-step stellar and \(1024\)-point thermodynamic
resolution, the 44 stable configurations have
\begin{equation}
 {\rm median}\left(
 \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
      {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}
 \right)=0.0697,
 \qquad
 q_{0.90}=0.205.
 \label{eq:broad-summary}
\end{equation}
All stable configurations retain an I--Love response smaller than C--Love;
the largest ratio is \(0.486\).  The two weakest cases occur at very low
compactness, \(C=0.030\) and \(0.045\).  Once configurations with
\(C<0.05\) are removed, all 41 remaining stable points satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
      {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}
 <0.231,
\end{equation}
and for \(C\ge0.08\) the maximum is \(0.218\).  The smooth and rough
background families give similar median ratios, \(0.064\) and \(0.072\),
respectively.

This result is deliberately broader than the small-polytrope study but
should not be confused with a nuclear-physics posterior over realistic EOSs.
It tests the functional geometry under large causal deformations of the
sound-speed profile.  Within that domain, the suppression is generic through
the neutron-star compactness range and weakens most clearly toward the
low-compactness limit.

A coarse \(512/256\) screening calculation initially assigned one rough
background a response ratio \(0.80\).  Recomputing the same configuration at
the production resolution gives \(0.218\).  This case demonstrates that
response ratios can be substantially more resolution-sensitive than the
baseline stellar observables for unusual EOS profiles.  All ensemble
statistics quoted above therefore use the fine calculation.

\section{Integrability and global reconstruction}
\label{sec:global}

A soft normal direction is a local statement.  A common quasi-universal
surface additionally requires the corresponding one-form to be approximately
integrable.  This is nontrivial only in the full three-dimensional observable
space: a line field in the two-dimensional I--Love plane is locally
integrable automatically.

\subsection{Frobenius obstruction}

At each stellar configuration we construct a three-dimensional domain patch
\[
x^i=(h_c,\alpha_{\rm hard},\alpha_{\rm soft}),
\]
where the EOS directions are the covariance-normalized right singular
vectors of the projected EOS response.  Centered derivatives of both
\(y^A=(\ln C,\ln\Ibar,\ln\Lamtwo)\) and the unit soft covector \(n_A\)
give
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial n_A}{\partial y^B}
    =
    \frac{\partial n_A}{\partial x^i}
    \left(
      \frac{\partial y}{\partial x}
    \right)^{-1\,i}{}_B .
\end{equation}
In the baseline Euclidean metric on logarithmic observables, the
codimension-one Frobenius condition becomes
\begin{equation}
    n\cdot(\nabla_y\times n)=0.
\end{equation}
We quote
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\rm F}
    =
    \frac{|n\cdot(\nabla_y\times n)|}
         {|\nabla_y\times n|}.
    \label{eq:eta-f}
\end{equation}

Because derivatives of the response eigenvector are more
resolution-sensitive than the first-order observables, this calculation is
performed with 4096 stellar steps and 2048 EOS reconstruction points.
Across nine patches---the undeformed reference, one smooth finite EOS
deformation, and one rough finite deformation, each at
\(h_c=0.12,0.20,0.28\)---we find
\begin{equation}
    {\rm median}(\eta_{\rm F})=1.23\times10^{-3},
    \qquad
    q_{0.90}=3.48\times10^{-3},
    \qquad
    \max\eta_{\rm F}=7.36\times10^{-3}.
    \label{eq:frobenius-result}
\end{equation}
A candidate \(3\times10^{-2}\) obstruction seen at half this resolution
collapses to \(1.23\times10^{-3}\) at the production
second-derivative resolution, so we do not interpret the coarse value as
physical non-integrability.

\subsection{Cross-validated scalar potential}

The local Frobenius result can be tested globally without fitting the
stellar observables themselves.  Since the \(\ln\Ibar\) component of the
soft covector is nonzero throughout the sampled domain, normalize the
one-form as
\begin{equation}
    d\ln\Ibar
    +A(\yvec)\,d\ln C
    +B(\yvec)\,d\ln\Lamtwo
    \simeq0 .
\end{equation}
If the field is integrable there exists a scalar
\begin{equation}
    F(\yvec)
    =
    \ln\Ibar+g(\ln C,\ln\Lamtwo)
    \label{eq:soft-potential}
\end{equation}
with
\[
\partial_{\ln C}g\simeq A,
\qquad
\partial_{\ln\Lamtwo}g\simeq B .
\]
We reconstruct \(g\) using only these gradient constraints; the observable
values themselves are never regression targets.

The dataset contains 79 stable configurations with \(C\ge0.05\), drawn from
16 smooth and rough latent-field backgrounds at six central enthalpies.
Polynomial potentials of degree two through four are tested.  A cubic is
retained as the reconstruction diagnostic because it improves over the
quadratic in both background cross-validation and EOS-family transfer,
whereas the quartic begins to overfit one family direction.

In four-fold cross-validation with complete EOS backgrounds held out, the
cubic reconstruction gives
\begin{equation}
    {\rm median}\,{\cal A}(\nabla F,n_{\rm soft})=0.9989
\end{equation}
and median held-out scatter
\begin{equation}
    \sigma(F)=8.48\times10^{-3}.
    \label{eq:potential-scatter}
\end{equation}
For a one-dimensional reconstruction constrained to depend only on
\(\ln\Lamtwo\), using the same gradient data,
\[
    \sigma(F_{I{\rm Love}})=1.98\times10^{-2}.
\]
The compactness-dependent soft surface therefore reduces the held-out scalar
scatter by \(57\%\).

The result transfers between qualitatively different EOS-background
families.  Training only on smooth backgrounds and testing only on rough
ones gives
\[
    \sigma(F)=8.89\times10^{-3},
    \qquad
    \sigma(F_{I{\rm Love}})=2.60\times10^{-2},
\]
while reversing the train/test families gives
\[
    \sigma(F)=9.27\times10^{-3},
    \qquad
    \sigma(F_{I{\rm Love}})=1.88\times10^{-2}.
\]
These are reductions of \(66\%\) and \(51\%\), respectively.  Since the
reconstruction is trained on the covector field rather than on the surface
values, this probes the common integrability of the local soft directions
rather than the flexibility of a direct fit through the stellar points.

Equations~\eqref{eq:frobenius-result}--\eqref{eq:potential-scatter}
provide complementary local and global tests of H3.  We use the polynomial
surface only as an integrability diagnostic here and do not interpret its
particular functional form as a new fundamental universal relation.

\section{Curvature and finite EOS scatter}
\label{sec:curvature}

We next test whether the leading finite departure from the \emph{local soft
tangent plane} is controlled by the second derivative of the
EOS-to-observable map.  At a fixed background configuration, let \(n_0\) be
the frozen soft covector and let \(d\) be a random unit-scale EOS
perturbation drawn from the declared GP metric.  Define the normal displacement
\begin{equation}
    R(s)
    =
    n_0\cdot
    \left[
      \yvec(a_0+s d)-\yvec(a_0)
    \right].
\end{equation}
The first- and second-order predictions are
\begin{align}
    R_1(s)
    &=
    s\,n_0 J d,
    \\
    R_2(s)
    &=
    s\,n_0 J d
    +
    \frac{s^2}{2}\,
    n_0 H[d,d].
    \label{eq:curvature-prediction}
\end{align}
The directional curvature \(n_0H[d,d]\) is evaluated with a centered
second difference at latent amplitude \(0.03\).

We use 48 random EOS directions at each of nine zero, smooth, and rough
background configurations.  Table~\ref{tab:curvature} summarizes the
relative RMSE of the two approximations against the exact finite
displacement.

higher orders become important.  This test does not by itself evaluate the
additional Hessian term associated with the curvature of the globally
reconstructed scalar \(F\).

\section{Controlled breakdown}
\label{sec:breakdown}

The broad latent-field ensemble above contains rough EOSs but does not isolate
a localized structural feature.  To test a controlled breakdown mechanism we
therefore impose a smooth, phase-transition-like softening window directly in
the latent sound-speed field.  The sound speed remains causal and the
thermodynamics are reconstructed exactly as in the baseline calculation.
The profile is
\begin{equation}
 \delta u(h)
 =
 -\frac{D}{2}
 \left[
  \tanh\frac{h-h_-}{\Delta h_{\rm edge}}
  -
  \tanh\frac{h-h_+}{\Delta h_{\rm edge}}
 \right],
 \label{eq:transition-window}
\end{equation}
where \(h_\pm\) define the softened interval.  We use 65 EOS nodes and
2048-point stellar and thermodynamic integrations for the refined cases.

For a window centered at \(h=0.16\), width \(0.04\), depth \(D=2\), and
edge scale \(0.008\), the minimum sound speed squared is
\(c_s^2\simeq0.0198\).  At \(h_c=0.30\) the compactness changes from
\(C=0.1713\) in the undeformed star to \(C=0.1065\).  More importantly, the
response geometry changes from
\begin{align}
 {\cal A}_{I{\rm Love}}
 &:
 0.9924\rightarrow0.9024,
 \\
 \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
      {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}
 &:
 0.1348\rightarrow0.4956,
 \\
 \mathcal R_{\rm N}
 &:
 0.0517\rightarrow0.0962.
 \label{eq:transition-rotation}
\end{align}
Thus the ordinary I--Love projection degrades by almost a factor of four in
relative RMS response, while the full observable-space response still
contains a pronounced soft normal direction.  The effect is therefore better
described as a rotation of the low-response manifold than as a disappearance
of low-dimensional structure.

A broader window of width \(0.08\), evaluated at \(h_c=0.36\), gives the same
qualitative result:
\[
 {\cal A}_{I{\rm Love}}=0.9260,
 \qquad
 \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}
      {\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}=0.4306,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{\rm N}=0.1203.
\]
The persistence of \(\mathcal R_{\rm N}\ll1\) is important: a localized EOS
feature can spoil the standard two-observable relation without destroying
the hierarchy of the full EOS response.

The origin of the rotation is visible directly in the EOS sensitivity
kernel.  For the first transition case, the covariance-weighted I--Love
normal variance increases from
\[
 5.99\times10^{-6}
 \quad\hbox{to}\quad
 5.10\times10^{-4},
\]
a factor of \(85.1\).  Of the positive excess in the direct nodal
I--Love sensitivity, \(90.2\%\) lies inside the imposed interval
\(h=0.14\)--0.18.  The excess peaks at \(h=0.155\) and has centroid
\(h=0.156\).  The breakdown of the I--Love projection is therefore localized
on the softened layer rather than being a global numerical displacement of
the star.

This controlled example supports H5 in a more specific form than originally
posed.  Sharp EOS structure need not eliminate the low-response manifold.
Instead, it can inject a localized EOS-sensitive direction into a particular
projection, rotating the globally soft covector away from the ordinary
I--Love normal.  The systematic scan below tests how this rotation depends on
the location, depth, and width of the softened layer.

\subsection{Systematic transition-location scan}

We scanned transition centers \(h_{\rm tr}=0.10,0.14,0.18,0.22\), widths \(0.04\) and \(0.08\), depths \(D=0.5,1,1.5,2\), and three stellar configurations at \(h_c=0.20,0.30,0.40\). Throughout these scans, ``stable'' denotes the positive-mass-slope screen \(dM/dh_c>0\); we do not solve a separate radial-mode eigenvalue problem. Of the 96 screening configurations, 69 pass this screen and satisfy \(0<C<0.35\). The strongest stable I--Love degradation moves with the stellar sequence rather than remaining at one absolute enthalpy: for fixed width \(0.08\) and depth \(1.5\), the extrema occur near
\begin{equation}
 (h_c,h_{\rm tr})
 =
 (0.20,0.10),\ (0.30,0.14),\ (0.40,0.18),
\end{equation}
corresponding to \(h_{\rm tr}/h_c\simeq0.45\)--0.50.

We refined these cases with 65 EOS nodes and 2048-point stellar and thermodynamic integrations. The response effect survives. The three refined I--Love/C--Love RMS ratios are \(0.756\), \(0.439\), and \(0.388\), while the corresponding full soft-to-hard RMS ratios remain \(0.063\), \(0.106\), and \(0.126\). More than \(90\%\) of the positive excess I--Love sensitivity remains localized on the softened interval in all three cases.

To separate the diffuse low-compactness endpoint from the more compact stars, we additionally scanned transition location in units of the central enthalpy, with fixed \(h\)-width \(0.25h_c\) and depth \(D=1.5\). For configurations with \(C\ge0.1\), the strongest stable rotation occurs at \(h_{\rm tr}/h_c=0.45\). At \(h_c=0.40\), this configuration has \(C=0.113\),
\begin{equation}
 {\cal A}_{I{\rm Love}}=0.922,\qquad
 \frac{\sigma_\perp^{I{\rm Love}}}{\sigma_\perp^{C{\rm Love}}}=0.447,\qquad
 \mathcal R_{\rm N}=0.135.
\end{equation}
The I--Love variance is enhanced by a factor \(56.7\), and \(93.3\%\) of the positive excess direct sensitivity lies within the imposed layer.

The enthalpy-coordinate background profile provides a physical location for this vulnerability. In the deformed star the softened layer is centered at
\begin{equation}
 r/R=0.453,\qquad m/M=0.759,
\end{equation}
and spans approximately \(0.386<r/R<0.535\). The same enthalpy interval in the undeformed reference configuration is centered at \(r/R=0.618\). These numbers are properties of the controlled \(\Gamma=2\) experiment, not a universal radius.

\subsection{Branch-safe cross-EOS control}

We therefore repeated the comparison with a branch-safe construction.  The
three reference polytropes \(\Gamma=1.70,1.85,2.00\) are matched at the
lower common baseline compactness \(C=0.14\), where their mass slopes are
positive:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dM}{dh_c}=0.707,\ 2.902,\ 2.685,
\end{equation}
respectively.  For each transition center
\(x\equiv h_{\rm tr}/h_c\), with width \(0.25h_c\), we sample the
compactness as a function of softening depth over \(0\le D\le3\), bracket
all roots of \(C(D)-C_{\rm final}\), and select the smallest-depth root
connected continuously to \(D=0\).  We retain a point only if
\(dM/dh_c>0\) at the target configuration and at 11 intermediate depths
along the deformation path.  The location grid covers
\(0.25\le x\le0.875\), the full range for which the nominal
\(0.25h_c\)-wide layer remains inside the star, and we test
\(C_{\rm final}=0.10\) and \(0.12\).

The depth-root construction is numerically well behaved.  The maximum
compactness residual at a refined root is \(1.1\times10^{-15}\).  Four
high-\(x\) cases possess two depth roots, demonstrating why a global
monotonicity assumption is unsafe; all four fail the positive-mass-slope
path screen and therefore do not enter the retained comparison.

The branch-safe result does not select an interior universal shell.  For
\(C_{\rm final}=0.10\), the largest retained I--Love/C--Love response
occurs at
\[
 x=0.475,\ 0.550,\ 0.600
\]
for \(\Gamma=1.70,1.85,2.00\), respectively.  For
\(C_{\rm final}=0.12\), the corresponding locations are
\[
 x=0.500,\ 0.650,\ 0.725.
\]
In every one of these six cases, this is also the largest \(x\) that
survives the positive-mass-slope path screen; the next grid point fails it.
The response is still increasing over the final retained points.  The
apparent maximum therefore tracks the screened branch boundary rather than
a preferred transition location.

Across the six boundary-adjacent retained points, the I--Love/C--Love RMS
ratio spans \(0.229\)--\(0.398\), the I--Love alignment spans
\(0.939\)--\(0.979\), and the full soft-to-hard RMS ratio remains
\(0.046\)--\(0.139\).  Mapping their layer centers into stellar
coordinates gives
\[
 0.368\le r_{\rm tr}/R\le0.476,
 \qquad
 0.235\le m_{\rm tr}/M\le0.675 .
\]
Neither enthalpy coordinate nor physical shell coordinate collapses to a
reference-EOS-independent value.  The robust conclusion is therefore the
localization capability demonstrated by the fixed-depth experiment, not a
universal vulnerable shell.
