\title{Neutron-star universal relations from equation-of-state response geometry}
\author{Ignacio Magana}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Neutron-star observables obey quasi-universal relations despite their strong
individual dependence on the equation of state (EOS). We formulate this
behavior as a property of the differential map from EOS function space to
relativistic stellar observables. For observable coordinates \(y^A\), EOS
coordinates \(a^i\), and a declared covariance \(C_{\rm EOS}^{ij}\) on EOS
perturbations, we study the induced response
\(G_{\rm EOS}^{AB}=J^A{}_iC_{\rm EOS}^{ij}J^B{}_j\), with
\(J^A{}_i=\partial y^A/\partial a^i\), after quotienting displacement along a
stellar sequence. In the space
\((\ln C,\ln\bar I,\ln\Lambda_2)\), the resulting normal response contains a
strongly suppressed mode that is recovered without imposing an empirical
I--Love fit. Across a broad causal latent-sound-speed ensemble, the median
soft-to-hard normal RMS ratio is \(0.020\), while the soft covector has median
cosine alignment \(0.998\) with the I--Love normal. The soft one-form is
approximately integrable: a high-resolution Frobenius diagnostic is
\(\lesssim7.4\times10^{-3}\) over the tested domain, and a scalar potential
reconstructed only from local response gradients generalizes across held-out
EOS backgrounds. Second-order curvature predicts finite normal
displacements with median relative error \(2.4\%\) at latent perturbation
amplitude \(0.1\), but loses quantitative accuracy for order-unity
deformations. Finally, localized sound-speed softening can strongly degrade
the ordinary I--Love projection while preserving the full soft-mode
hierarchy; the added sensitivity is concentrated on the softened layer.
These results separate quasi-universality into local EOS insensitivity,
integrability, and nonlinear curvature, and provide a differential
description of both its persistence and controlled breakdown.
\end{abstract}
