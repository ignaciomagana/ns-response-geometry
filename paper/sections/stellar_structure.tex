\section{Relativistic stellar structure}
\label{sec:stars}

\subsection{Background configurations}

For a static spherical star,
\begin{equation}
    ds^2
    =
    -e^{2\nu(r)}dt^2
    +
    \left(1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2
    +
    r^2d\Omega^2 .
\end{equation}
The Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff equations are
\begin{align}
    \frac{dm}{dr}
    &=4\pi r^2\epsilon,
    \\
    \frac{dp}{dr}
    &=
    -\frac{(\epsilon+p)(m+4\pi r^3p)}
    {r(r-2m)} .
    \label{eq:tov}
\end{align}
We use relativistic enthalpy
\begin{equation}
    h(p)=\int_0^p\frac{dp'}{\epsilon(p')+p'}
    \label{eq:enthalpy}
\end{equation}
as the integration coordinate. Since
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dh}{dr}
    =
    -\frac{m+4\pi r^3p}{r(r-2m)},
\end{equation}
the background system integrated in the code is
\begin{align}
    \frac{dr}{dh}
    &=
    -\frac{r(r-2m)}{m+4\pi r^3p},
    \label{eq:drdh}
    \\
    \frac{dm}{dh}
    &=
    4\pi r^2\epsilon\frac{dr}{dh}.
    \label{eq:dmdh}
\end{align}
The stellar surface is at \(h=0\), and
\begin{equation}
    M=m(R),\qquad C=M/R.
\end{equation}

The enthalpy equations are singular at the exact center.  We start a small interval
\(\Delta h\) below \(h_c\) using the regular series
\begin{align}
    r_0^2
    &=
    \frac{3\Delta h}{2\pi(\epsilon_c+3p_c)}
    +\mathcal O(\Delta h^2),
    \\
    m_0
    &=
    \frac{4\pi}{3}\epsilon_c r_0^3
    +\mathcal O(r_0^5).
    \label{eq:center-series}
\end{align}
The production fixed-step integration presently uses
\(\Delta h/h_c=10^{-4}\); convergence with respect to both this offset and
the number of integration steps is part of the validation described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}.

\subsection{Quadrupolar tidal response}

We use the standard relativistic static \(l=2\) tidal equations
\cite{hinderer2008love,postnikov2010love}.  Defining
\begin{equation}
    y(r)\equiv r\frac{H'(r)}{H(r)},
\end{equation}
the first-order equation is
\begin{equation}
    r\frac{dy}{dr}
    +
    y^2+F(r)y+r^2Q(r)=0,
    \label{eq:y-tidal}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    F(r)
    =
    \frac{1-4\pi r^2(\epsilon-p)}
    {1-2m/r},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
    Q(r)
    &=
    \frac{4\pi}
    {1-2m/r}
    \left[
        5\epsilon+9p+\frac{\epsilon+p}{c_s^2}
        -\frac{6}{4\pi r^2}
    \right]
    \nonumber\\
    &\quad
    -
    4
    \left[
        \frac{m+4\pi r^3p}
        {r^2(1-2m/r)}
    \right]^2.
    \label{eq:Q-tidal}
\end{align}
In enthalpy coordinates we use \(dy/dh=(dy/dr)(dr/dh)\), with regular
central condition
\begin{equation}
    y(r\rightarrow0)=2+\mathcal O(r^2).
\end{equation}

For an EOS with nonzero surface energy density, the logarithmic derivative
must include the surface-density jump \cite{postnikov2010love},
\begin{equation}
    y_R
    =
    y(R^-)
    -
    \frac{4\pi R^3\epsilon_s}{M}.
    \label{eq:y-surface}
\end{equation}
The Love number is then
\begin{align}
k_2
&=
\frac{8C^5}{5}(1-2C)^2
\left[2+2C(y_R-1)-y_R\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad\times
\Big\{
2C[6-3y_R+3C(5y_R-8)]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad
+4C^3[13-11y_R+C(3y_R-2)+2C^2(1+y_R)]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad
+3(1-2C)^2
[2-y_R+2C(y_R-1)]
\ln(1-2C)
\Big\}^{-1}.
\label{eq:k2}
\end{align}
We report the dimensionless tidal deformability
\begin{equation}
    \Lamtwo=\frac{2}{3}k_2C^{-5}.
    \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Moment of inertia}

The moment of inertia is calculated at first order in uniform rotation using
Hartle's slow-rotation formalism \cite{hartle1967rotation}.  Let
\(\bar\omega(r)=\Omega-\omega(r)\) and
\begin{equation}
    j(r)
    =
    e^{-\nu(r)}
    \sqrt{1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}}.
\end{equation}
The frame-dragging equation is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dr}
    \left(
      r^4j\frac{d\bar\omega}{dr}
    \right)
    +
    4r^3\frac{dj}{dr}\bar\omega
    =0.
    \label{eq:hartle}
\end{equation}
Only \(d\ln j/dr\) is needed in the first-order system, so the arbitrary
normalization of \(\nu\) never enters. Near the center,
\begin{equation}
    \bar\omega(r)
    =
    1
    +
    \frac{8\pi}{5}(\epsilon_c+p_c)r^2
    +\mathcal O(r^4),
    \label{eq:w-center}
\end{equation}
where the overall normalization is arbitrary.

Matching to
\begin{equation}
    \bar\omega_{\rm ext}(r)
    =
    \Omega-\frac{2J}{r^3}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
    J=\frac{R^4}{6}\bar\omega'(R),
    \qquad
    \Omega
    =
    \bar\omega(R)
    +
    \frac{R}{3}\bar\omega'(R),
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
    I
    =
    \frac{R^4\bar\omega'(R)}
    {6\bar\omega(R)+2R\bar\omega'(R)},
    \qquad
    \Ibar=\frac{I}{M^3}.
    \label{eq:I}
\end{equation}

\subsection{EOS coordinates and perturbations}

The final response calculation requires EOS perturbations that approach a functional description while satisfying thermodynamic stability and causality by construction. The baseline representation uses a latent field \(u(h)\) mapped to the sound speed,
\begin{equation}
    c_s^2(h)=\sigma[u(h)],
    \qquad
    \sigma(u)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}},
    \label{eq:cs-map}
\end{equation}
with stricter bounds applied explicitly if required. The latent field is expanded as
\begin{equation}
    u(h)=u_0(h)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_b}a_i\phi_i(h).
    \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
The basis \(\phi_i\) is a numerical representation only. Physical conclusions must converge as \(N_b\) and the basis family are varied.

For the initial response calculation, \(u_0(h)\) is taken from a causal
reference EOS above a matching enthalpy \(h_{\rm match}\).  Below the match,
the reference EOS is retained unchanged.  Above it, the perturbed sound speed
is used to reconstruct the thermodynamics rather than perturbing pressure and
energy-density tables independently.  From the definition of enthalpy and
\(c_s^2=dp/d\epsilon\),
\begin{align}
    \frac{dp}{dh}
    &=
    \epsilon+p,
    \label{eq:dpdh-eos}
    \\
    \frac{d\epsilon}{dh}
    &=
    \frac{\epsilon+p}{c_s^2(h)}.
    \label{eq:dedh-eos}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:dpdh-eos}--\eqref{eq:dedh-eos} are initialized with
\(p(h_{\rm match})\) and \(\epsilon(h_{\rm match})\) from the reference EOS.
Thus every finite perturbation used in the Jacobian corresponds to a
thermodynamically consistent barotrope, with \(0<c_s^2<1\) enforced by the
latent logistic map.

For the production response coordinates we use nodal values of the latent
perturbation,
\begin{equation}
    a_i \equiv \delta u(h_i),
    \qquad
    u(h)=u_0(h)+\delta u(h),
    \label{eq:nodal-coordinates}
\end{equation}
with \(\delta u(h)\) linearly interpolated between the nodes.  This choice is
not intended to assign physical meaning to individual nodes.  Its purpose is
to make basis refinement well defined: if \(C_{\rm EOS}(h,h')\) is a target
functional covariance, then the finite-dimensional metric is simply its
evaluation on the nodes,
\begin{equation}
    C_{\rm EOS}^{ij}
    =
    C_{\rm EOS}(h_i,h_j).
    \label{eq:nodal-covariance}
\end{equation}
Increasing the number of nodes therefore refines the representation of one
fixed stochastic process rather than changing the normalization or meaning
of the perturbation coefficients.

Our baseline target metric is the squared-exponential kernel
\begin{equation}
    C_{\rm EOS}(h,h')
    =
    \sigma_u^2
    \exp\left[
      -\frac{(h-h')^2}{2\ell_h^2}
    \right].
    \label{eq:eos-covariance}
\end{equation}
We also use Mat\'ern-\(3/2\) and exponential kernels to test sensitivity to
rougher EOS perturbations.  The amplitude \(\sigma_u\) scales the absolute
linear-response variance but cancels from ratios of response scales formed
with the same EOS metric.  The correlation length \(\ell_h\), in contrast,
changes which density scales are allowed to vary coherently and is therefore
a physical robustness parameter.

For Stage 1 validation we additionally use two analytic EOS families. A
constant-energy-density star provides the exact Schwarzschild-interior
background solution. A relativistic \(\Gamma=2\) polytrope supplies the
Newtonian \(n=1\) limits of \(k_2\) and \(I/(MR^2)\). Neither benchmark EOS
defines the final EOS metric. The first response calculations use smooth
causal relativistic polytropes only as controlled reference points; broad
nonparametric EOS ensembles enter after derivative, node-refinement, and
metric-robustness tests.
